**A curated evidence base for the discussion paper and presentation**

Sources drawn from MBB and Big 4 consulting, big tech research, university/academic papers, and regulators — mapped to the themes in `ai-comparative-advantage-strategy.md`. Each entry notes the headline finding and where it strengthens the argument.

## Tier 1 — The load-bearing sources

These five carry the most weight for the core thesis (*advantage comes from metabolism, not tool access*). If you read nothing else, read these.

### 1. MIT NANDA — "The GenAI Divide: State of AI in Business 2025"
- **Link:** [Report PDF](https://mlq.ai/media/quarterly_decks/v0.1_State_of_AI_in_Business_2025_Report.pdf) · [Fortune coverage](https://fortune.com/2025/08/18/mit-report-95-percent-generative-ai-pilots-at-companies-failing-cfo/)
- **Headline:** Despite US$30–40B of enterprise investment, **95% of GenAI pilots deliver no measurable P&L impact**. The 5% that succeed embed AI into high-value workflows with memory and learning loops. The failure is not model quality — it's the "learning gap": tools that don't learn from workflows, and organisations that don't learn from tools.
- **Use for:** The core thesis (§11) and metabolising (§8). This is the single best statistic for "pilots are not capability."

### 2. BCG — "Where's the Value in AI?" (2024)
- **Link:** [bcg.com](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2024/wheres-value-in-ai) · [Press release](https://www.bcg.com/press/24october2024-ai-adoption-in-2024-74-of-companies-struggle-to-achieve-and-scale-value)
- **Headline:** Survey of 1,000 CxOs: **74% of companies show no tangible value from AI**; only 4% operate "value engines." Leaders put **twice the investment into people and process as into technology** (the "10-20-70" rule: 10% algorithms, 20% tech/data, 70% people and processes), and the companies extracting most value run the most ambitious upskilling programs.
- **Use for:** Comparative advantage (§1) and skill gap (§6). The 70% people-and-process figure is the cleanest consulting evidence that the operating model is the moat.

### 3. McKinsey — "Superagency in the Workplace" (Jan 2025)
- **Link:** [mckinsey.com](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/tech-and-ai/our-insights/superagency-in-the-workplace-empowering-people-to-unlock-ais-full-potential-at-work)
- **Headline:** 92% of companies plan to increase AI investment, but **only 1% of leaders describe their company as "mature"** on deployment. Employees are readier than leaders think: employees report 3× more AI usage than their C-suite estimates. "The challenge is not a technology challenge — it is a business challenge."
- **Use for:** Skill gap (§6), operating model (§7), metabolising (§8). The employee-vs-leader perception gap is a strong provocation for a senior-leader audience.

### 4. Dell'Acqua et al. (Harvard/BCG) — "Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier" (2023)
- **Link:** [HBS working paper](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=64700) · [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4573321)
- **Headline:** Field experiment with 758 BCG consultants. Inside the AI frontier: **+12% tasks completed, +25% speed, significantly higher quality**. Outside the frontier: AI users were **19 percentage points more likely to be wrong**. Introduced the "centaur" and "cyborg" working styles.
- **Use for:** Skill gap (§6) and governance (§2). The jagged-frontier concept is the best academic frame for why judgement-focused human oversight matters — AI is superhuman at some tasks and confidently wrong at adjacent ones.

### 5. Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond — "Generative AI at Work" (NBER w31161; QJE 2025)
- **Link:** [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161) · [QJE published version](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article/140/2/889/7990658)
- **Headline:** 5,179 customer-support agents with an AI assistant: **+14% productivity overall, +34% for novice/low-skill workers**, minimal gain for top performers. AI disseminated the best practices of the most able workers; retention and customer sentiment improved.
- **Use for:** Customer service (§3) and skill gap (§6). Note the nuance: in this setting AI *compressed* the skill gap (lifting novices), while the jagged-frontier study shows top-performer dynamics differ by task type — good discussion tension for your "three groups" framing.

### §3 Customer service

- **Brynjolfsson et al. (above)** is the canonical agent-assist evidence: productivity, retention *and* customer sentiment all improved when AI supported the human rather than replacing them.
- **McKinsey agentic banking work (above)** frames the next horizon: when *customers* bring their own agents, service becomes machine-to-machine negotiation — journeys, not chatbots.
- Accenture — [Banking in the Age of Generative AI](https://www.accenture.com/us-en/insights/banking/generative-ai-banking): **73% of US bank employees' time** has high potential to be impacted (39% automation, 34% augmentation); relationship managers and credit analysts are *empowered*, not displaced.
- **The Klarna arc** — the canonical over-automation case: AI handled two-thirds of chats in month one (work of ~700 agents; resolution 11 min → under 2), then quality broke on complex/emotional cases and Klarna rehired humans, the CEO conceding it "cut too far." Lesson: AI plus a guaranteed human path; measure resolution by case complexity, not deflection averages. [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/quickerbettertech/2025/05/18/business-tech-news-klarna-reverses-on-ai-says-customers-like-talking-to-people/) · [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91468582/klarna-tried-to-replace-its-workforce-with-ai)

### §7 Operating model

- **McKinsey — "Scaling gen AI in banking: choosing the best operating model"**: directly on point. >90% of institutions at McKinsey's banking forum have some centralised gen-AI function; the archetypes split ~20% highly centralised / ~30% centrally led, BU-executed / ~30% BU-led, centrally supported. McKinsey's finding: **more centralised steering correlates with getting use cases to production faster in the early phase** — evolving toward federation as maturity grows. [mckinsey.de](https://www.mckinsey.de/industries/financial-services/our-insights/scaling-gen-ai-in-banking-choosing-the-best-operating-model)
- Accenture — [AI implications for leadership, culture and operating models in financial services](https://bankingblog.accenture.com/ai-implications-financial-services) and [Agentic AI and the future of work in FS](https://bankingblog.accenture.com/agentic-ai-future-of-work): Talent Reinventors are 4.4× more likely to have an adaptable workforce.

### §8 Metabolising

- MIT GenAI Divide (Tier 1) — the "learning gap" is essentially the metabolism argument with data.
- McKinsey Superagency (Tier 1) — only 1% mature; the bottleneck is leadership and rewiring, not employee willingness.
- BCG 10-20-70 (Tier 1) — 70% of the work is people and process change.

### §9 Global comparators

- **Evident AI Index** — the single best artefact for this section: independent benchmark of the 50 largest banks across Talent, Innovation, Leadership, Transparency. 2025 edition: **JPMorganChase #1 (fourth consecutive time), Capital One #2, RBC #3 — and CommBank #4, the top non-North-American bank and among the highest AI-talent density anywhere.** Proof that an Australian bank can compete globally on AI maturity — and a ready-made scorecard for "who do we benchmark against." [evidentinsights.com](https://evidentinsights.com/ai-index/) · [2025 Index summary](https://evidentinsights.com/bankingbrief/heres-the-2025-evident-ai-index/)
- Europe leg: the APRA/ASIC letters (above) plus EU AI Act analyses give the regulated-adoption comparison.
- East Coast leg: JPMorgan's own [AI maturity communications](https://www.jpmorganchase.com/about/technology/blog/jpmc-evident-25) and the McKinsey banking operating-model work.
- West Coast leg: Microsoft Work Trend Index + Anthropic Economic Index as frontier-signal artefacts.

### §10 Talent / floating experts

- Accenture talent-reinvention finding (4.4× adaptability, 7.2× internal fills) supports fluid internal deployment.
- Microsoft "Work Charts" concept — outcome-driven teams drawing on the right mix of humans and agents — is the big-tech articulation of the floating model.
- WEF Future of Jobs: 50% of employers plan to transition staff from declining to growing roles — the floating layer is the mechanism that makes that real for scarce technical talent.

## Suggested citation strategy for the conversation

Three numbers to anchor the room:

1. **95% / 5%** — MIT: 95% of GenAI pilots deliver no P&L impact; the 5% that work embed AI into workflows that learn. *(Why metabolism matters.)*
2. **10-20-70** — BCG: 10% of the effort is algorithms, 20% tech and data, 70% people and process. *(Where the investment should go.)*
3. **#4** — Evident: CommBank is already the world's #4 bank for AI maturity. *(Australia can lead — the question is whether the rest of the sector treats that as a benchmark or an outlier.)*

And one regulatory anchor: **APRA's April 2026 letter** — the governance agenda is now explicit; the strategic choice is whether to meet it as a floor or build it into a paved road.
